Drinking Water Treatment

Drinking water treatment is defined as the process of purifying water from various sources, including groundwater, surface water, and rainwater, through methods such as coagulation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection to make it safe for human consumption. This treatment can occur at municipal water treatment plants or through point of use systems that utilize various filtration and disinfection techniques.

Application 02

Industrial Water Treatment

Industrial water treatment encompasses the purification of incoming raw water and the cleanup of facility wastewater to protect machinery, ensure product quality, and meet environmental regulations.

Key Treatment Streams

  • Makeup/Raw Water Treatment: Prepares natural water sources (rivers, groundwater) for specific tasks like boiler feed or cooling towers by removing minerals, hardness, and suspended solids to prevent scaling and corrosion.
  • Wastewater/Effluent Treatment: Cleans used water from manufacturing before safe discharge or reuse through physical, chemical, and biological methods.

Core Processes and Technologies

  • Physical & Mechanical: Screening, sedimentation, and media filtration remove large debris, grit, and suspended particles.
  • Chemical Treatment: Coagulation, flocculation, and pH neutralization group fine particles together and balance water chemistry.
  • Biological Treatment: Activated sludge systems and microbial processes break down organic matter, nitrogen, and phosphorus.
  • Advanced Separation: Reverse Osmosis (RO), ion exchange, and Nanofiltration remove dissolved salts and heavy metals for ultra-pure requirements.

Application 03

Waste Water Treatment

Waste water treatment is a multi step process that removes physical, chemical, and biological contaminants from used water so it can be safely returned to the environment or reused.

Main Stages of Treatment

  • Preliminary Treatment: Screens and filters out large objects like plastics, rags, and grit to protect plant equipment.
  • Primary Treatment: Uses sedimentation tanks to slow water down, allowing heavier solids to settle as sludge and oils to float as scum.
  • Secondary Treatment: Employs microorganisms and aerobic bacteria in aeration tanks to break down dissolved and biological organic matter.
  • Tertiary Treatment: Uses chemical disinfection, ultraviolet (UV) light, or filtration to remove remaining nutrients (like nitrogen and phosphorus) and pathogens.

Application 04

Boiler Water Treatment

Internal treatment of boiler feed water is the process of adding specific chemicals directly into the boiler system to react with residual impurities, prevent scale and corrosion, and condition sludge.

Even when water goes through external softening first, small amounts of hardness-causing minerals and gases still enter the boiler. Internal treatment acts as the final shield to protect metal surfaces and maintain safe operations.

Core Goals

  • Prevent Scale: Stop hard mineral deposits from sticking to hot boiler tubes.
  • Stop Corrosion: Remove leftover oxygen and balance water alkalinity.
  • Control Foaming: Prevent bubbles and boiling water carryover into the steam lines.
  • Manage Sludge: Keep suspended solid particles loose so they can be flushed out.

Main Internal Treatment Methods

  • Colloidal Conditioning: Agents like kerosene, tannin, or agar-agar are added to low-pressure boilers. They coat tiny scale particles, stopping them from sticking to the walls so they can be blown down easily.
  • Phosphate Conditioning: Trisodium phosphate is added to react with calcium and magnesium. This turns them into a soft, non-adherent sludge (calcium phosphate) that settles at the bottom and leaves through the bottom blowdown.
  • Calgon Conditioning: Sodium hexametaphosphate (Calgon) is used to bind calcium ions into a water-soluble complex. This prevents solid scale from forming even under high heat.
  • Carbonate Conditioning: Sodium carbonate is added in lower-pressure systems to turn calcium and magnesium into removable insoluble precipitates.
  • Oxygen Scavenging: Chemicals like hydrazine or sodium sulfite are injected to absorb leftover dissolved oxygen gas, preventing rust and pitting in the feed lines and drum.
  • Anti-Foaming Agents: Compounds like castor oil or polyamides are added to break up surface foam and stop water droplets from mixing into the generated steam.

Cooling Water Treatment

Cooling water treatment is the process of managing water quality in industrial cooling systems to prevent scaling, corrosion, fouling, and biological growth.

Makeup Water Pre-Treatment

Raw source water (from wells or rivers) is conditioned before entering the system.

  • Filtration: Removes suspended solids and debris using sand or ultrafiltration screens.
  • Softening / Demineralization: Uses ion exchange or reverse osmosis to remove hardness minerals like calcium and magnesium that cause scale.

Internal Chemical Treatment

  • Scale Inhibitors: Chemicals like phosphonates and polymers stop minerals from sticking to heat exchangers.
  • Corrosion Inhibitors: Products like azoles or phosphates form a protective shield on metal pipes.
  • Biocides: Oxidizing (chlorine, bromine) and non-oxidizing chemicals kill bacteria, algae, and fungi to prevent biofouling.
  • pH Control: Acids or bases are added to keep the water pH in a safe, non-corrosive range.

Blowdown and Monitoring

  • Blowdown (Bleed): A portion of concentrated water is drained regularly and replaced with fresh makeup water to control total dissolved solids (TDS) buildup from evaporation.
  • Routine Testing: Operators continuously check conductivity, pH, and microbial levels to adjust chemical feed rates.

Application 06

Reverse Osmosis (RO) Water Treatment

A reverse osmosis (RO) water treatment plant purifies water by forcing it through a semi-permeable membrane under high pressure to remove dissolved salts, minerals, and impurities.

The RO treatment process operates through three main phases: pre-treatment, membrane separation, and post-treatment.

Pre-Treatment Process

Pre-treatment prepares the raw water and protects the delicate RO membranes from clogging or damage.

  • Raw Water Intake: Water is pumped from a source like groundwater, a river, or municipal lines into a feed tank.
  • Multimedia / Sand Filter: Water passes through a sand or multi-bed filter to trap large suspended solids, dirt, and turbidity.
  • Activated Carbon Filter: This filter absorbs chlorine, chloramines, and organic chemicals that can ruin an RO membrane.
  • Antiscalant Dosing: Chemicals are added to prevent mineral scaling (such as calcium and magnesium buildup) on the membrane surface.
  • Cartridge Filter: A fine microfilter catches tiny leftover particles just before the water enters the high-pressure pump.

Reverse Osmosis (RO) Membrane Separation

This is the core stage where purification actually takes place.

  • High-Pressure Pump: A strong pump pressurizes the pre-treated water to overcome the natural osmotic pressure of the solution.
  • Pressure Vessels & Membranes: Pressurized water enters vessels containing spiral-wound semi-permeable membranes.
  • Permeate and Reject Streams: The membrane allows pure water molecules to pass through (called permeate or product water) while trapping up to 99% of dissolved salts and contaminants. These trapped impurities flow out in a separate wastewater stream (called reject or concentrate water).

Post-Treatment and Storage

The final stage prepares the purified water for safe consumption or industrial use.

  • Disinfection: Treated water may pass through a ultraviolet (UV) sterilizer or ozone system to kill any lingering bacteria or viruses.
  • Remineralization: Essential minerals can be added back if the water is meant for drinking and needs a better taste or balanced pH.
  • Storage Tank: Purified water is kept in a clean storage tank until it is pumped out for distribution.
